Molly Holzschlag

Wrote or co-authored 35 books on web design, including The Zen of CSS Design. Led the Web Standards Project (2004–2006) and championed accessibility and education. (1963–2023)

Molly Holzschlag was born in Brooklyn, New York, in 1963. She became one of the web's best-known teachers, and many people knew her as the "Fairy Godmother of the Web." She wrote or co-wrote 35 books on web design and open standards. The best known is The Zen of CSS Design, written with Dave Shea in 2005 and based on the CSS Zen Garden project.

Standards and accessibility

From 2004 to 2006 she led the Web Standards Project, which pressed browser makers such as Microsoft, Opera, and Netscape to support web standards properly. At the W3C she was an invited expert on the CSS, HTML, and GEO working groups, and she chaired the CSS Accessibility Community Group.

Accessibility was at the center of her work. In 2011 she taught HTML5 and ARIA for Knowbility, an accessibility nonprofit, with a focus on inclusive design.

Teaching and community

  • Taught web courses at the University of Arizona, New School University, and other schools.
  • Created Open Web Camp, a free Silicon Valley event, and led it for its first five years (2009–2013).
  • Named one of the Top 25 Women on the Web by Webgrrls San Francisco (1998).
  • Received a Net Award for Outstanding Contribution (2015) and the O'Reilly Web Platform Award (2016).

She lived with aplastic anemia in her later years and spoke openly about the cost of health care. She died at home in Tucson, Arizona, on 5 September 2023, at age 60.
