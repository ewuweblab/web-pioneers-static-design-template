Tim Berners-Lee

Invented the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989, creating HTML, URLs and HTTP. Founded the W3C in 1994 and co-founded the Open Data Institute.

Tim Berners-Lee was born in London in 1955 and studied physics at The Queen's College, Oxford. In March 1989, while working at CERN, he wrote a proposal for a system to help researchers share and link documents across computers. That proposal grew into the World Wide Web.

By the end of 1990 he had built the pieces that made it work: the first web server, the first browser (called WorldWideWeb, written on a NeXT computer), and the first website, which went live on 20 December 1990. He invited outside collaborators in August 1991, and CERN later made the technology freely available. It was never patented and never charged for, which is a big part of why the web spread so fast.

Keeping the web open

  • 1994: Founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT to develop open web standards.
  • 2004: Knighted for services to the global development of the Internet.
  • 2009: Launched the World Wide Web Foundation with Rosemary Leith.
  • 2012: Co-founded the Open Data Institute with Nigel Shadbolt. At the London Olympics opening ceremony he typed "This is for everyone."
  • 2017: Received the 2016 ACM A. M. Turing Award, often called the Nobel Prize of computing.
  • 2018: Co-founded Inrupt to support Solid, a project that gives people control over their own data.

In 2025 he published a memoir, This Is for Everyone, named after the Olympics message.
