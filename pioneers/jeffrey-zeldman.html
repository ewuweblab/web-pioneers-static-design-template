Jeffrey Zeldman

Co-founded A List Apart and the Web Standards Project. Wrote Designing with Web Standards and co-founded the An Event Apart conference.

Jeffrey Zeldman was born in Queens, New York, in 1955. He trained as a fiction writer, briefly worked as a reporter, and spent about ten years as an advertising copywriter before he switched to web design in 1995. That year he launched zeldman.com, where early tutorials like "Ask Dr. Web" taught a generation of designers how to build sites.

Making the case for standards

In the late 1990s, browsers each did things their own way, and designers had to build several versions of every site. In 1998 Zeldman co-founded the Web Standards Project with George Olsen and Glenn Davis to push browser makers to support the W3C's standards. He led the group from 1999 to 2002. The same year he co-launched A List Apart, a magazine for people who make websites.

His 2003 book Designing with Web Standards argued that separating structure (HTML) from presentation (CSS) made sites faster, cheaper to maintain, and more accessible. The book went through three editions; Ethan Marcotte co-wrote the third in 2009.

Building a community

  • 1999: Founded the design studio Happy Cog. Later he started Studio Zeldman in 2016.
  • 2005: Co-founded the An Event Apart conference with Eric Meyer.
  • 2010: Started A Book Apart, which publishes short books for web designers.

He also co-hosts The Big Web Show podcast.
